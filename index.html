01 — What Is This?

The Big Picture

A live, dynamic, evolving case competition — part escape room, part battle royale, part consulting simulation. Teams are hired consulting firms tasked with saving a fictional company called NovaMart — a mid-size retail chain bleeding money while simultaneously facing a PR crisis.

The twist? The case changes every round. New information drops, new stakeholders enter, and old solutions break — just like real consulting.

02 — The Method Behind the Madness

ACT 01

"THE BURNING PLATFORM"

Root Cause Analysis · Problem Definition

Teams receive a deliberately vague crisis brief about NovaMart. Revenue has dropped 23% in 6 months. The CEO wants answers — but no one told you why.

  • Teams must submit a Problem Tree — going at least 3 levels deep using the Why → Why → Why method. Shallow answers score zero.
  • Each team starts with 3 Clarifying Question Tokens they can use to ask the host (playing "the client") one question each. Spend them now or save them.
  • Structure required: Surface Problem → Why? → Root Cause → Why? → Actual Root Cause
  • Recommended frameworks: MECE Issue Tree, 5-Whys, Fishbone Diagram
⚡ The Challenge

The brief is intentionally ambiguous. Teams that jump to "fix revenue" without diagnosing the real problem will get demolished in Act 4 when the Veto Bomb drops.

ACT 02

"STAKEHOLDER WARS"

Stakeholder Mapping · Influence vs Interest

A deck of Stakeholder Cards is placed face-down on each table. Every 2 minutes, a new card flips — revealing a new stakeholder with hidden agendas, fears, and leverage.

  • Teams live-update their Influence vs. Interest Grid as each stakeholder is revealed
  • For each card: document what they want, what they fear, and whether they're a blocker or enabler
  • Sample stakeholders: Store Manager Union, Tier-1 Investor, Local Community Leaders, Customer Advocacy Groups, Regulators
  • Teams must constantly revise earlier assessments as new stakeholders change the power dynamics
🃏 The Wildcard Stakeholder

One random card in every team's deck is a ridiculous wildcard — e.g., "A viral food blogger who reviewed NovaMart's in-store cafe." The team that best argues why this stakeholder matters earns a bonus multiplier. This teaches that no stakeholder is truly irrelevant.

ACT 03

"GUESTIMATE GLADIATOR"

Market Sizing · Structured Estimation · Calibrated Confidence

Buzzer round. All teams compete simultaneously. Host reads guestimate questions one by one — first team to lock in earns the right to present their logic. Speed matters, but so does structure.

  • Sample questions: "How many grocery bills does NovaMart process per day across 200 stores?"
  • Sample questions: "What % of customers will download NovaMart's loyalty app in Year 1?"
  • Sample questions: "How much revenue can NovaMart recover by optimising shelf space in 50 stores?"
  • Confidence Betting: Before answering, teams bet 1–3 Insight Tokens. Correct = double your tokens. Wrong = lose them. Teaches calibrated confidence.
  • You are judged on the quality of your logic chain, not just the final number
🧠 The Unanswerable Question

One question has no right answer — judged purely on thinking quality. This teaches the most important consulting lesson: the process is the product, not the output.

ACT 04

"THE WAR ROOM"

Executive Communication · Pyramid Principle · Synthesis

Final 2-minute pitch to the full panel. No bullet-point slides — verbal only, or one visual. Teams must use the Pyramid Principle: Answer first, then support it. Plus one devastating curveball 30 seconds before they go up.

  • Structure required: Lead with the Answer → 3 Key Arguments → Data from the Game
  • Each team also submits a One-Pager (literally one A4 sheet) — because in real consulting, the CEO reads the one-pager, not the deck
  • Judged on: clarity, stakeholder coverage, structure, data usage, adaptability
  • Top 3 teams from Acts 1–3 get a 30-second advantage: they see the curveball 30s earlier
💥 The 30-Second Curveball

Just before presenting, teams receive a final update: "NovaMart's biggest competitor just announced a 30% price cut." They must address this with zero additional prep time. This is the defining moment of the competition — the teams who've built adaptive solutions all game will shine here.

05 — Before the Event

Organiser Checklist

📄
NovaMart Case Brief

2-page fictional company profile. Include financials, market context, and the PR issue. Keep it deliberately incomplete.

🃏
Stakeholder Card Deck

Print 10–12 unique cards per table. Include one Wildcard per deck. Each card: name, title, concern, hidden fear.

🟡
Insight Tokens

Poker chips work perfectly. 5 tokens per team to start. Used for confidence betting in Act 3.

🔴
Veto Cards

One red card per judge. Bright, physical, dramatic — the physical slam is half the fun.

📋
Judge Scoring Rubric

Pre-built rubric per act. Distributed to judges 30 minutes before the event starts.

💥
Curveball Updates

One unique update per team for Act 5. Can vary across teams for surprise. Seal in an envelope.

🎙️
The Client Host

Your most charismatic person plays "the NovaMart client." Must be able to improvise responses to Clarifying Token questions.

⏱️
Visible Timer

A large countdown timer visible to all teams. Time pressure is a feature, not a bug.
